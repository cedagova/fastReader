fastReader 1.7.0 (versionCode 9) — Android 8.0+.

Install it over 1.6.0 or earlier; do not uninstall first. Your books, your place
in each of them and your settings are kept.

## New

- **Your Reader account's books appear on the shelf.** Sign in under
  **Settings → Reader account** and the shelf shows what that account holds
  alongside what is on this phone. A book the account has *and* this device has
  is **one row**, not two — matched by the file's own checksum, showing the
  device's cover, author and your place in it. A book only the account has is an
  **account-only row**: title, author and "In your Reader account. Not on this
  device.", with **Download and open** under it.
- **Remove a book from your account, with Undo.** The account-only row offers
  **Remove from account**; the confirmation says plainly that the book leaves
  the Reader account on **every device signed in to it**, and that the file here
  and your place in it are untouched. An Undo bar follows for about eight
  seconds — the same window a device removal already gives you — and taking the
  removal back reaches the backend too.
- **Opening and finishing an account book is recorded for the account.** For a
  book your account already holds, FastReader tells the Reader API that you
  opened it, when you last opened it, and when you finished it. Nothing is
  recorded for a book that is only on this phone.
- **Add a book on this phone to your account.** A device book now offers **Add
  to account library**. It asks before anything happens, and the question says
  what it is asking for: the file's bytes go to your Reader account and are
  kept there. It shows how large the file is and the largest your account
  accepts — both read from the backend, neither built into the app. Until you
  tap **Add**, nothing about that book has left the phone. The upload then runs
  on the row with a progress bar and its own Cancel; when it finishes, the book
  appears as an account book and can be read on your other devices. Killing the
  app part-way through picks the same upload back up rather than starting a
  second one, and adding the same file twice does not make a second book. A
  refusal — too large, unsupported, copy-protected — shows the backend's own
  words and leaves the book on this phone exactly as it was.
- **Download an account book and read it offline.** **Download and open** on an
  account-only row fetches that book through a one-off address the Reader API
  issues for it, shows the transfer on the row with its own Cancel, checks the
  file's SHA-256 against the one your account holds, and only then opens it.
  Nothing opens before that check passes: a file that arrives wrong is refused
  with the reason — as are a full disk, a lost connection and a backend that
  will not hand the book over — and in every one of those cases the book is
  still in your account and still on the shelf. Once the copy is here it is an
  ordinary book: it opens in **airplane mode**, keeps your place, shows its
  cover, and can be read on a phone that has been offline since.
- **Free a downloaded copy without leaving the account.** A downloaded book's
  row offers **Remove downloaded copy**. The confirmation says how much it
  frees, and it is the only removal in FastReader that deletes a file — it
  deletes FastReader's own private copy and nothing else. The book stays in
  your Reader account on every device, your place in it is kept, and fetching
  it again picks up where you left off. That row has no other removal, so there
  is no way to drop it and leave the bytes behind.
- **Signing out keeps your downloaded books.** A copy you have downloaded is an
  ordinary book on this phone: after you sign out it is still there, still
  opens, and can still be removed. Signing back in to the same account shows it
  as an account book again — matched, as always, by the file's own checksum.
- **One refresh control for both halves.** Pull the shelf's refresh and it
  rescans your folders *and* asks the account for its current list.

## Changed

- **The statement below says what now leaves this device.** Since 1.6.0 it said
  your books, positions and settings "stay on this device and are never sent".
  That is no longer the whole truth: your account's book list, and the reading
  status and last-opened time of the books it holds, go to the Reader API. The
  statement now says exactly that and no more — no book file is sent, a book
  only on this phone is never named to the Reader API, and your reading
  positions, reading speed, other settings and crash reports still never leave.
  It also says the one new thing FastReader keeps *here*: a copy of your
  account's book list, in the app's private storage — and that signing out does
  **not** delete it, so signing back in to the same account picks up where it
  left off. Only uninstalling FastReader or clearing its data removes it.
- **And it now says what an upload sends.** Two promises are retired with the
  add-to-account action, because they stopped being true: "no book file is ever
  sent" and "a book that is only on this device is never named to the Reader
  API". Both are replaced by the same claim made conditional on the thing the
  app actually requires — a book file is sent only for a book **you** add that
  way, and a book only on this phone is never named to the Reader API **until
  you add it**. The statement also names what adding sends besides the file:
  its name, its size, its format and its checksum. Nothing else changed: no
  reading position, no reading speed, no setting and no crash report leaves.
- **And it now says what a download fetches, and what it leaves here.** Two
  sentences are added and none is retired: downloading a book is the first
  thing FastReader does that moves a whole file *towards* this phone, so "that
  is all that leaves this device" is still true word for word. The statement
  now says which request fetches the book — a one-off address the Reader API
  issues for a book your account already holds — and that your account's
  sign-in is **never** given to the storage the file comes from. It also names
  the copy among the things FastReader keeps here, and says the three things
  that matter about it: it is in the app's private storage, it is left out of
  this device's backup and of a transfer to a new phone, and **Remove
  downloaded copy** deletes it without taking the book out of your account.
- **Signed out, nothing changed.** With no Reader account signed in, the shelf,
  Settings and the reader are the 1.6.0 app, and the account library sends
  nothing at all. A book you downloaded while signed in stays, and stays
  readable.

## Unchanged

Books, positions and settings survive the update. Reading itself keeps the same
cues, the same chapter pause and the same Spanish interface — a downloaded
account book reads exactly like a book from your own folders, because that is
what it is. FastReader still runs no background work: it has no scheduler, no
receiver and no new permission, and a download happens because you asked for
one. The account
session, when there is one, is still kept encrypted in a place the device's
backup and phone-to-phone transfer never read. The release gate still proves
that the APK asks for exactly the internet permission plus Android's own
broadcast self-permission, and that no cleartext connection is allowed.

FastReader is for personal use and distributed by link. It is not on any store —
see issue #33 for why that is still true.

## What stays on this device

Rewritten for the account library, for adding a book to it and for downloading
one from it. The sentences about what the Reader API is told, about what an
upload sends, and about what a download fetches and leaves behind are new; the
rest is the 1.6.0 statement.

FastReader has the internet permission and uses it for one thing only:
the optional Reader account under Settings. Nothing is sent unless you
use that account. When you do, your email address, the code or
password you type and the account's session go to the Reader identity
provider and the Reader API. FastReader also asks the Reader API which
books your account already holds, and for those books only it tells
the Reader API that you opened one, when you last opened it, whether
you have finished it, and when you take one out of your account or put
it back. When you choose Add to account library for a book on this
device and confirm, FastReader asks the Reader API what kinds and
sizes of file your account accepts and then sends that book's file,
its name, its size, its format and its checksum to the Reader API and
its storage, where your account keeps them; nothing about that book is
sent before you confirm. When you choose Download and open for a book
your account already holds, FastReader asks the Reader API for a
one-off address for that book's file and fetches the file from that
address; the request names only a book your account already has, and
the account's sign-in is never given to the storage the file comes
from. That is all that leaves this device: a book file is sent only
for a book you add that way, a book that is only on this device is
never named to the Reader API until you add it, and your reading
positions, your reading speed, your other settings and any crash
report stay on this device and are never sent. The account session is
kept encrypted on this device, outside its backup, and is removed when
you sign out. Check for updates only hands a web address to your
browser, and your browser makes that request. Your books stay in the
folders you chose; on this device FastReader keeps only its own list
of them, your reading positions, your settings, small cover thumbnails
and, once you sign in, a copy of your account's own book list, a note
of any book you are part-way through adding to it and the file of any
account book you have downloaded, in its private storage. A downloaded
copy is a file in that private storage like the rest: it is left out
of this device's backup and of a transfer to a new phone, and Remove
downloaded copy on the book's row deletes it from this device without
taking the book out of your Reader account. That copy of the account's
list is not deleted when you sign out: it stays in that private
storage, so signing in to the same account again picks up where it
left off, and only uninstalling FastReader or clearing its data
removes it. If the app stops unexpectedly it also keeps one short
report about what went wrong in that private storage: the app version,
this device's model, its Android version and where in the code it
stopped, with no part of any book in it — the next launch offers that
report to you once, and it goes nowhere unless you share it and pick
an app to send it to. None of that is included in this device's backup
or in a transfer to a new phone, so a reinstall or a new phone starts
with an empty library. When another app opens a book in FastReader and
does not give lasting permission to read it, that book is not added to
your list and no permission to it is kept; only your place in it is
remembered.
